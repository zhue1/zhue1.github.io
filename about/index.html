About · Miami → Gainesville, FL

I want to build something that matters before I leave this world.

I'm Eric, a CS senior at the University of Florida and the founder of Orbi Labs. This is the honest version of how I got here, and why I build.

Where I come from

My parents never got a day off. That's my starting line.

I grew up in Miami in a Chinese family that spoke Mandarin at home. We weren't wealthy. My parents worked at a family-owned restaurant every day, almost never taking a break, so I could have chances they never had.

We rarely went anywhere. I've been to China once, with my mom the summer after fifth grade, and to New York to see family. That's the whole map so far. I grew up knowing exactly what my opportunities cost the people who gave them to me.

Sixth grade

I taught myself to code, because nobody was going to hand it to me.

At the start of sixth grade I opened Roblox Studio out of pure curiosity and started writing Lua. While my classmates were just playing games, I was learning loops, events and how a game actually works underneath.

That curiosity didn't stop. By high school I'd built and released an entire multiplayer RPG by myself: the combat, the save system, the enemies and the world. 7,000+ lines of code, no team and no tutorial for the hard parts.

The gap I couldn't ignore

People work hard in the gym and still don't get results.

Lifting became a big part of my life, and I kept seeing the same thing: people showing up day after day, giving real effort, and not getting the results they came for. The fix exists, a good coach, but coaching is expensive. I ran into that wall myself.

So I'm building Ora: an AI training coach grounded in real, up-to-date research, so anyone can get coaching that's personal, honest and built on evidence rather than guesswork. I'm the only engineer behind it, with 570+ merged pull requests and counting.

Why I keep going

It feels less like a career and more like a calling.

There's a feeling I've carried for a long time: that I was put here to do something that matters, and that I can't waste it. Some of the few things that can move me to tears are lonely elderly people and sick children. Seeing people suffer, I made myself a promise: reduce as much of it as I can.

Ora is the first step: healthier, stronger people, and, with their consent, training data that can push the science of lifting forward. To move faster on my own, I built EricOS, a system that lets me work like a small team.

I want my parents to finally rest and see the world they spent their lives working for. And I want to leave this world better than I found it.
